# Customer Churn & Retention Analytics

**Portfolio Project 02 | SQL · Excel · Power BI**

## Business question
Which customers are leaving, what recurring charges are associated with those customers, and which segments should a retention team investigate first?

This project walks through a practical analytics workflow: raw customer records, data cleaning, KPI calculations, segment comparisons, and a management-facing report.

## Dataset at a glance
The demonstration dataset contains 808 raw records. Eight duplicate customer IDs are removed, leaving 800 curated customer records, including 269 records labelled as churned. That gives an overall churn rate of 33.6%.

Monthly charges across the curated customer base total ₹4,98,800. The monthly charges associated with churned customers total ₹1,77,690. These figures are calculated from synthetic data and should be treated as illustrative, not as real company revenue or a forecast.

## Key finding
In this sample, month-to-month customers have a much higher churn rate than customers on longer contracts:

| Contract type | Customers | Churned | Churn rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Month-to-month | 400 | 232 | 58.0% |
| One year | 240 | 32 | 13.3% |
| Two year | 160 | 5 | 3.1% |
| **Total** | **800** | **269** | **33.6%** |

This is a useful segment to investigate in a real business. It is not proof that contract length causes churn: the labels in this synthetic dataset are rule-generated and some relationships are built into the data.

## Core metrics
- **Churn rate:** churned customers divided by total unique customers.
- **Retention rate:** customers not labelled churned divided by total unique customers.
- **Monthly recurring revenue (MRR):** sum of recorded monthly charges across the curated base.
- **Monthly charges associated with churn:** sum of monthly charges for customers labelled as churned. This is an exposure measure, not a forecast.
- **Segment churn rate:** churned customers within a segment divided by all customers in that segment.

## Tools and workflow
1. Inspect the raw CSV and validate customer IDs and field types.
2. Clean duplicates and check missing values and category ranges.
3. Use SQL to calculate overall KPIs and compare churn by contract and payment method.
4. Use Excel to reconcile summary figures and review the executive report.
5. Use the supplied Power Query and DAX files to build an interactive Power BI report.

A finished native Power BI `.pbix` file is not included; the report needs to be assembled in Power BI Desktop using the supplied guide and measures.

## Limitations
- All customer records are synthetic; no real customer data is included.
- The churn label is generated by a deterministic rule using several customer attributes. Segment patterns therefore demonstrate analysis techniques rather than independent evidence of cause.
- The dataset is a snapshot and does not contain real cancellation dates, so it cannot support a genuine monthly churn trend or cohort-retention curve.
- Revenue associated with churned customers is not the same as a forecast of future revenue loss.
- No churn-prediction model is trained or validated in this project.

## Next steps for a real deployment
Replace the sample data with approved customer records, establish a reliable cancellation date and churn definition, validate the billing fields against finance records, and measure retention campaigns with a suitable control group. Report incremental retained customers and net benefit rather than counting offers sent.
